# Help Desk Hero: teacher guide

**Lesson:** 2 · Basic Troubleshooting
**Suggested grades:** 2 to 4
**Play time:** 15 to 25 minutes for all nine tickets

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the game, students can:

1. Observe a computer setup carefully and name clues that point to a problem (icons, lights, cables, on-screen messages).
2. Link a symptom to its most likely cause.
3. Choose a safe response, and recognise when a problem must be reported to an adult.
4. Describe a problem clearly and specifically ("The monitor cable looks loose"), not vaguely ("It's broken").
5. Apply the four Safety First habits: stay calm, ask for help, don't touch wires, be patient.

## Running the session

**Before (5 min).** Ask: "What would you do if your computer stopped working in the lab?" Collect answers on the board without judging.

**Play (15 to 20 min).** Students play individually or in pairs. In pairs, one student is the "reporter" who must say the teacher message aloud before clicking it. Encourage the Read aloud button for early readers.

**After (10 min).** Return to the board answers and sort them into "safe to do myself" and "tell a teacher".

## Discussion questions

- Why did the game want you to tell the teacher even when you knew how to fix the cable?
- What makes a good help desk message? Compare "It's not working" with "The mouse wire has come out."
- Why does clicking again and again make a busy computer slower?
- Which clue was hardest to spot? Where will you look first next time?

## Extension: unplugged role-play

Set up a real (switched off) computer with one "problem" (for example, a loose mouse cable that the teacher prepares). One student plays the worried student, one plays the help desk hero. The hero must look, think, then report to the teacher using a full sentence.

## Quick assessment

| Level | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| Beginning | Guesses causes; tries to fix wires or equipment. |
| Developing | Finds the key clue with a hint; chooses safe actions on a second try. |
| Secure | Finds clues independently; chooses safe actions first time; reports problems specifically. |

The certificate screen shows points, first-time diagnoses, first-time safe choices and badge counts, which map onto this rubric. Students can type their name and print it.
